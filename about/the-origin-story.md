# The Origin Story

Honestly, the idea for Sākṣī didn't come from a desire to write complex software. It all started when I ([Rajnish Kumar](about-the-developer.md)) watched a video online that just didn't sit right with me.

## The Spark

I was watching this video where a motorcyclist was supposedly getting beaten up by some police officers. But the craziest part was that the person recording the whole thing was forced to delete the video right there from their phone.

It just made me think. Here is someone with actual proof of something wrong happening, but just because they are intimidated, the evidence is gone forever.

I started wondering, *Why isn't there an app that just stops you from deleting a video for a few days?* Like, even if someone forces you, the file stays. But at that time, I just thought about it and moved on.

## The Idea Returns in July 2026

Fast forward to July 2026. The student protests were happening (the [CJP students protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Delhi_Jantar_Mantar_protests)). I came across this interview video where two girls were talking to a journalist/reporter.

They were explaining how the police had misbehaved with them, doing inappropriate things. The heartbreaking part? The girls had recorded the abuse on their phones, but the police forcefully made them delete the videos.

Hearing that just triggered the memory of that older video. It really hit me hard. This kind of stuff happens way too often. You see it online all the time—videos of corruption, government officials misusing their power, people getting harassed. And so many of those recordings just get deleted because the person recording gets threatened.

I thought, why does the truth get deleted just because someone yells at you to delete it?

## The Initial Concept

So, I decided to actually try and build something. At first, I thought, I'll just make an app that locks files so nobody can delete them for a set number of days.

But then I realized, if someone is pointing a stick at you telling you to delete the video, and the app says "You can't," they'll just smash the phone.

So I changed my mind. What if I make a camera app that secretly saves a full copy of the video somewhere else, but leaves a normal copy in the gallery? That way, you can just delete the one in the gallery to satisfy whoever is threatening you, but the real copy is safe.

But wait, what if they just uninstall my camera app? If the data is inside the camera app, uninstalling it ruins everything.

I needed a better way to hide the files. And that's when a memory from my childhood gave me the answer. But I'll talk about that in the [Evolution of the Ecosystem](evolution-of-the-ecosystem.md).
